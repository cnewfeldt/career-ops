# Story Bank — Master STAR+R Stories

This file accumulates your best interview stories over time. Each evaluation (Block F) adds new stories here. Instead of memorizing 100 answers, maintain 5-10 deep stories that you can bend to answer almost any behavioral question.

## How it works

1. Every time `/career-ops oferta` generates Block F (Interview Plan), new STAR+R stories get appended here
2. Before your next interview, review this file — your stories are already organized by theme
3. The "Big Three" questions can be answered with stories from this bank:
   - "Tell me about yourself" → combine 2-3 stories into a narrative
   - "Tell me about your most impactful project" → pick your highest-impact story
   - "Tell me about a conflict you resolved" → find a story with a Reflection

## Stories

### [Curriculum Design] XR Media Camp -- Meta Quest VR Curriculum
**Source:** Report #001 -- XR Terra -- Curriculum Developer, Unity VR Development
**S (Situation):** Centre for Digital Media needed a 5-day XR camp curriculum for youth with no prior Unity experience.
**T (Task):** Design end-to-end curriculum for Meta Quest 2, targeting ages 12-17.
**A (Action):** Built modular daily lessons progressing from Unity basics to VR deployment on Quest devices. Structured around mini-projects building to a final capstone.
**R (Result):** Delivered 6 successful cohorts; students completed working VR projects by day 5.
**Reflection:** Modular design lets you adapt pacing per cohort without redesigning the whole course. Younger learners need shorter feedback loops.
**Best for questions about:** curriculum design, modular learning, VR education, student experience, project-based learning

### [Audience Adaptation] Teaching Across the Full Spectrum
**Source:** Report #003 -- Anthropic -- Developer Education Lead
**S (Situation):** Taught across wildly different audiences at SFU -- undergraduates, graduate students, youth camp participants, and corporate professionals.
**T (Task):** Adapt the same core concepts (3D graphics, XR, immersive tech) for each audience's level, goals, and context.
**A (Action):** Used different pedagogical approaches: hands-on project-based for youth, theory + practice for grad students, portfolio-focused for undergrads. Redesigned course materials for each audience rather than recycling.
**R (Result):** Successfully delivered courses spanning IAT102 (intro design) through IAT872 (graduate real-time engines) and youth camps for ages 12-17.
**Reflection:** The same concept needs 3 different explanations for 3 different audiences. What changes is the "why this matters to you" framing. This is the core skill of developer education -- knowing your audience.
**Best for questions about:** audience awareness, adaptability, teaching philosophy, developer education strategy
